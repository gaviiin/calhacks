# Names

The brief: an agentic platform that tracks hardware and the money it costs and predicts both; the car is the ledger; every part is followed from fitting to scrap; it has to work beyond F1, in aviation, fleets, rail and heavy equipment. The founder likes the feel of **Galvanize** and **Temper**: metal, process, strength.

What a name has to do here:
1. It should sound like metal treated by a process, so it says "hardware", not "fintech".
2. It should carry a second meaning about measure, record or control, because the product is an accountant.
3. A judge should be able to say it after hearing it once, in a 3-minute demo.
4. It should not collide with a big product in fintech, audit software or motorsport.

The trademark and domain notes are my own read, not a search. Check them before anything goes on a slide.

## Top three

1. **Anneal**
2. **Temper**
3. **Witness Mark**

## Recommendation: Anneal

- **It is the thesis in one word.** Annealing takes stress out of metal, and the product's core input is stress events on parts. The near-homophone with "annual" ties it to the annual cost cap without spelling it out.
- **It has the feel the founder asked for.** It is in the same family as Galvanize and Temper (heat, metal, process) but without their problems. Galvanize collides with an audit-software brand, the exact market this product sells into. Temper is a staffing platform in Europe and also means losing your cool.
- **It travels.** Aviation MRO, rail and heavy equipment all anneal metal. Nothing in the word is about racing.
- **It is easy to say after one hearing** ("uh-NEEL"). The demo opener can teach it in four words: "Anneal: stress out, value in."
- **The brand system comes with it.** The glyph uses real temper colours (straw, bronze, blue) on a short bar. That sits next to the life gradient (sky, amber, red) as a sibling, not a copy, and keeps Williams blue as the main brand colour.

Lockup for the top bar: the glyph (20×6 tri-band bar) + `ANNEAL` (Barlow Condensed 800, 18px, +0.04em, navy) + a muted divider + `Williams Racing` (DM Sans 600 11px caps). The tagline goes on the title slide only, not in the app: **"Every part, fitting to scrap, in the books."**

If the founder wants a word everyone already knows, use **Temper** with a modifier (`Temper Parts`) to step around the staffing brand. **Witness Mark** is the choice if the pitch leans on controls and audit more than on hardware.
