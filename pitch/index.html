Finance runs on modules. This one is for the hardware.

Seen

Telemetry sees car 23 go from 315 to 0 km/h. A tripwire in code, not a model: over 100 km/h to under 25 with the brake on, or telemetry frozen on OpenF1's sentinel values. Race control never announces a retirement, so the desk does not wait for it.

Hardware

Jev reads the impact and the parts it likely hit, each with a probability, in about a hundred milliseconds. Likely parts are marked damaged at once and replaced when the garage confirms. The power unit was not hit, but it now runs out before the season ends, past its allocation: a grid penalty, found the moment it happened.

Engineering call

The garage confirms heavy damage in one click. The Team Principal sees each way out, priced side by side and checked against the clock. No recommendation: the call is theirs.

Race call

None for car 23: it does not start. When Colapinto crashes in the race, the strategist gets stay out, pit now, the other compound or the same stop under green, each priced in positions, points, prize money and cap. The agent marks what the team actually did, and recommends nothing.

Next steps

Execution follows the choice. Internal, reversible steps run on their own; anything outward or irreversible waits for its owner, and the module refuses anything else.

Books

Balanced entries, each saying when the cap counts it. The response plan names each line's closest case in the team's own history: the Chief of Staff approves the plan once, every line with a precedent posts on its own whatever its size, and a line the team has never seen waits for the Head of Cost Cap.

Season

Ten thousand seasons re-run after every change, with the same random draws each time, so a change between two runs is the evidence and not noise. The engineers and the CFO see the same numbers tonight, not next week.

Seal and true-up

Race-day finance is provisional, and Anneal says so. On Sunday night the weekend is sealed under one Merkle root, with a brief for each role that cites the row behind every figure; a check rejects any number the snapshot does not hold, and nothing goes out until the CFO approves. Weeks later the true-up compares the seal with the actuals and recalibrates the price book.

Every feature, and why it is built this way.

F1 is a strange accounting world: the asset races, breaks and is rebuilt overnight, and the regulator counts a cost the day a part is fitted. Each piece is engineered for that.

Inside the hardware process learner.

Anneal learns how this team runs its car from what the team did before. Nothing is configured: everything it does on its own, it earned from evidence, and a person can take it back.

Match an event against the team's own history

Every past session becomes a case with a small feature vector. A new event takes its seven nearest cases. When at least three score 0.85 or more and 80% of them agree, their outcome is the protocol and it books the money on its own authority, naming the cases. These are the 93 real cases São Paulo learns from: Williams' 2024 races before it, and two press-sourced crashes.

Earn a licence

A policy decides without a person only when its record says it can. With n cases of which k ended the way it says, the lower bound of the 90% Wilson interval must clear 0.75, with at least five cases. A policy below that is a shadow: it watches, and its effects are drawn dashed.

The loop

  1. HistoryThe team's 2024 sessions, normalised the same way the live tape is. Each era learns only from races before it, so a replay never matches its own future.
  2. Cases and policiesGroups with enough cases become Ripple-Down Rule policies with their cases as precedents. Numbers come from code; Claude only reviews whether a draft generalises.
  3. Response planAfter a crash, every hardware operation and entry is a line with its closest precedent. Lines at 0.8 or above execute once the Chief of Staff approves the plan; a line with none waits for the Head of Cost Cap.
  4. CorrectionA person's answer becomes a stored case carrying the band they booked. Three alike corrections, or a policy contradicted more than 20% of the time, and the learner drafts a child rule.

The four day-one questions

World
Where does it act? In the team's systems of record, through one door: the ERP accepts a race entry only when pit-wall authorized it, so an agent holding the gateway key still cannot post around the rule.
Time
What does it remember next quarter? Precedents and licensed policies learned from the team's own sessions and every correction, kept in eras so a replay never learns from its own future.
Team
Who does it work with? People by role: Chief Mechanic, Team Principal, Sporting Director, Head of Strategy, Chief of Staff, Head of Cost Cap, CFO. Seats are bound to identities; agents are refused every person's decision.
Limits
What may it do, and can it prove why? The rules live in the database: one gate, one audit row per decision, refusals committed rather than thrown. Racing calls are priced, never recommended.

Your judging questions

Real enough?
Real OpenF1 timing, telemetry and race control, real 2024 results, the real Financial Regulations. A messy corner with an answer key: the seal against what the press reported.
Hire it?
It fills the race accountant's seat: 2,549 events, five touches by people, three crashes booked, sealed and briefed.
Something new?

Systems of record

OpenF1, Jolpica, FIA documents, the ERP journal, spares stores, freight. Anneal integrates with them and never replaces them.

The gateway

System of work

Part life, incidents, response plans, questions, entries, seats, the audit trail. Agents and people share it.

pit-wall is the backend, not a store.
The rules that must hold no matter which process misbehaves live in the module.

  • One door, one audit rowEvery decision that moves money or speaks for a person goes through gate(): the caller is resolved from the connection's identity, never from an argument; the change applies or is refused; exactly one race_audit row lands in the same transaction. A refusal is committed, not thrown.
  • The database authorizes postingsThe desk asks with request_race_posting; the module answers authorized, or held for a named role, from the entry, the estimate behind it and the response plan's precedent. A sign-off or a plan approval re-runs the decision in the same transaction.
  • People are seatsgrant_seat binds an identity to a role and a name. The publisher's identity runs the agents and never counts as a person; the person who signed an estimate off cannot also approve its posting.
  • The database keeps timeA question's deadline is a scheduled reducer: when it comes due the module applies the stated default, even with no agent running. The replay's pace maps race time to the wall clock.
  • Measured inside the transactionrace_latency is timed from desk read to commit inside the reducer; race_online is kept by the connect and disconnect hooks. Every view shows both.
  • Every view, the same rowsEach role's view is a live subscription to the same tables, so the engineers and the CFO can never disagree about what happened.

What nobody else built

A module for hardware assets. Finance modules follow the money; this one follows the part, and the money follows it into every other ledger.

  • Wear moves the forecast, not the booksThe cap excludes depreciation, so life sets when a part is fitted, and fitting is when its cost counts.
  • Crashes seen before race controlA telemetry tripwire, because OpenF1 never announces a retirement.
  • Autonomy as a licenceA 90% Wilson bound over the policy's own record; 3 of 3 is still a shadow.
  • Edit the plan in plain words"Hand carry the floor instead." The plan holds, the agent turns the words into operations, rebuilds the plan against the team's history and records what it did with each sentence; then it executes.
  • No amount thresholdsHistory decides who looks at a line, not its size: the same part, booked the same way, at a price the team has paid before posts on its own; a familiar part at an unfamiliar price waits.
  • Grid penalties as moneyA power unit heading past its allocation is found the moment a crash makes it likely.
  • Ask only when it paysA person is asked when a wrong call would cost more than the question, with a deadline the database enforces.
  • Priced, never recommendedRepair, spare tub or withdraw; stay out or pit: every option in positions, points, prize and cap, the choice left to its owner.
  • Sealed and trued upA Merkle root on Sunday night, briefs that cite every row, and a true-up that recalibrates the price book.
